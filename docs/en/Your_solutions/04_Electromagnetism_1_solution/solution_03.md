## 3. Electrostatic Equilibrium

**Given**

- $q_1 = +4\,\mathrm{C}$
- $q_2 = +9\,\mathrm{C}$
- Distance between them: $2\,\mathrm{m}$
- Test charge: $q_3 = +1\,\mathrm{C}$

**Analysis**

Let the equilibrium point be at distance $x$ from $q_1$.  
Then its distance from $q_2$ is $2-x$.

Since all charges are positive, the only possible equilibrium point is **between** $q_1$ and $q_2$.

For equilibrium, the two repulsive forces on $q_3$ must have equal magnitude:

$$
k\frac{q_1 q_3}{x^2} = k\frac{q_2 q_3}{(2-x)^2}
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
k\frac{4 \cdot 1}{x^2} = k\frac{9 \cdot 1}{(2-x)^2}
$$

Cancel $k$:

$$
\frac{4}{x^2} = \frac{9}{(2-x)^2}
$$

Take square roots:

$$
\frac{2}{x} = \frac{3}{2-x}
$$

Solve:

$$
2(2-x) = 3x
$$

$$
4 - 2x = 3x
$$

$$
5x = 4
$$

$$
x = 0.8\,\mathrm{m}
$$

**Result**

The equilibrium position is

$$
\boxed{0.8\,\mathrm{m}\text{ from }q_1}
$$

or equivalently

$$
\boxed{1.2\,\mathrm{m}\text{ from }q_2}
$$

**Check**

The equilibrium point must be closer to the smaller charge.  
Here it is closer to $q_1 = 4\,\mathrm{C}$, so the result is consistent.
