## 2. Electric Potential

**Given**

- Charges at the corners of a square: $+1\,\mathrm{C}$, $-2\,\mathrm{C}$, $+3\,\mathrm{C}$, $-4\,\mathrm{C}$
- Side length: $a = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$

**Analysis**

The distance from the center to each corner is

$$
r = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Electric potential is a scalar, so the contributions add directly:

$$
V = k \sum \frac{q_i}{r}
= \frac{k}{r}(1 - 2 + 3 - 4)
$$

$$
1 - 2 + 3 - 4 = -2
$$

So,

$$
V = \frac{k(-2)}{1/\sqrt{2}} = -2\sqrt{2}\,k
$$

Using $k \approx 9.0 \times 10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$,

$$
V \approx -2\sqrt{2}(9.0 \times 10^9)
\approx -2.55 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{V}
$$

**Result**

$$
\boxed{V = -2\sqrt{2}\,k \approx -2.55 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{V}}
$$

**Check**

Potential is scalar, so no vector decomposition is needed.
