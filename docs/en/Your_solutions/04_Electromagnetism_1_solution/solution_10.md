## 10. Lorentz Force Acting on a Wire

**Given**

- Wire length:

$$
L = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$

- Current:

$$
I = 10\,\mathrm{A}
$$

- Magnetic field:

$$
B = 0.5\,\mathrm{T}
$$

**Analysis**

The magnetic force on a straight current-carrying wire is

$$
F = BIL\sin\theta
$$

Substitute the constant part first:

$$
BIL = (0.5)(10)(2.0) = 10
$$

So,

$$
F = 10\sin\theta
$$

---

### a) $\theta = 90^\circ$

$$
F = 10\sin 90^\circ = 10(1) = 10\,\mathrm{N}
$$

$$
\boxed{F = 10\,\mathrm{N}}
$$

---

### b) $\theta = 45^\circ$

$$
F = 10\sin 45^\circ = 10\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
$$

$$
F = 5\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 7.07\,\mathrm{N}
$$

$$
\boxed{F = 5\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 7.07\,\mathrm{N}}
$$

---

### c) $\theta = 0^\circ$

$$
F = 10\sin 0^\circ = 0
$$

$$
\boxed{F = 0\,\mathrm{N}}
$$

**Result**

$$
\boxed{F(90^\circ)=10\,\mathrm{N}}
$$

$$
\boxed{F(45^\circ)=7.07\,\mathrm{N}}
$$

$$
\boxed{F(0^\circ)=0\,\mathrm{N}}
$$

**Check**

The force is maximum at $90^\circ$ and zero at $0^\circ$.  
If your result does not follow this, the angle dependence is wrong.
