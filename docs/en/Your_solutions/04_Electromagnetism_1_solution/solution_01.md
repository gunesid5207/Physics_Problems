## 1. Coulomb's Law

**Given**

- Four charges: $+1.0\,\mathrm{C}$ at the corners of a square  
- Side length: $a = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$  
- Charge at center: $q_c = -2.0\,\mathrm{C}$  

**Analysis**

All four corner charges are identical and symmetrically placed.

- Distance from center to each corner:

$$
r = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\mathrm{m}
$$

- Each charge exerts an attractive force on the central charge.
- The four forces have equal magnitude.
- Directions are along diagonals toward each corner.

By symmetry:

- Horizontal components cancel.
- Vertical components cancel.

**Result**

$$
\boxed{\vec F_{\text{net}} = 0}
$$

Magnitude:

$$
\boxed{0\,\mathrm{N}}
$$

Direction:

No direction (net force is zero due to symmetry).

**Sanity check**

Even though each individual force is non-zero, symmetry forces complete cancellation.  
If you didn’t get zero here, your vector decomposition is wrong.
