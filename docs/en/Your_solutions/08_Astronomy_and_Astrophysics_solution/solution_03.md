# 3. Microgravity

Given:

$$
h = 400\,\mathrm{km}
$$

$$
R_E = 6378\,\mathrm{km}
$$

$$
M_E = 5.97 \times 10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg}
$$

$$
G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/kg^2}
$$

The distance from Earth's center is:

$$
r = R_E + h
$$

$$
r = 6378 + 400
$$

$$
r = 6778\,\mathrm{km}
$$

Convert to meters:

$$
r = 6.778 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The gravitational acceleration is:

$$
g = \frac{GM_E}{r^2}
$$

Substitute:

$$
g =
\frac{(6.67 \times 10^{-11})(5.97 \times 10^{24})}
{(6.778 \times 10^6)^2}
$$

$$
g \approx 8.67\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

---

## Why Astronauts Feel Weightless

The gravity at the ISS is still strong:

$$
g_{\mathrm{ISS}} \approx 8.67\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

This is only slightly smaller than Earth's surface gravity:

$$
g_{\mathrm{Earth}} \approx 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

Astronauts feel weightless because the ISS and the astronauts are both in continuous free fall around Earth. They are falling toward Earth, but because they also have a large sideways velocity, they keep missing Earth and remain in orbit.

---

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{
g_{\mathrm{ISS}} \approx 8.67\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
}
$$

Astronauts experience weightlessness because they and the ISS are in free fall together, not because gravity is zero.
