# 2. Resistors

We have exactly three resistors:

$$
R = 1\,\Omega
$$

We list all possible unique equivalent resistances.

---

## 1. All in Series

For series connection:

$$
R_{\text{eq}} = 1 + 1 + 1
$$

$$
\boxed{R_{\text{eq}} = 3\,\Omega}
$$

---

## 2. All in Parallel

For parallel connection:

$$
\frac{1}{R_{\text{eq}}}
=
1 + 1 + 1
$$

$$
\frac{1}{R_{\text{eq}}} = 3
$$

$$
\boxed{R_{\text{eq}} = \frac{1}{3}\,\Omega}
$$

---

## 3. Two in Series, Then Parallel with Third

First combine two resistors in series:

$$
R = 1 + 1 = 2\,\Omega
$$

Now place this in parallel with the third resistor:

$$
\frac{1}{R_{\text{eq}}}
=
\frac{1}{2} + 1
$$

$$
\frac{1}{R_{\text{eq}}}
=
\frac{3}{2}
$$

$$
\boxed{R_{\text{eq}} = \frac{2}{3}\,\Omega}
$$

---

## 4. Two in Parallel, Then Series with Third

First combine two resistors in parallel:

$$
\frac{1}{R}
=
1 + 1
$$

$$
R = \frac{1}{2}\,\Omega
$$

Now place this in series with the third resistor:

$$
R_{\text{eq}} = \frac{1}{2} + 1
$$

$$
\boxed{R_{\text{eq}} = \frac{3}{2}\,\Omega}
$$

---

## Final Answers

All unique equivalent resistances are:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{1}{3}\,\Omega,\;
\frac{2}{3}\,\Omega,\;
\frac{3}{2}\,\Omega,\;
3\,\Omega
}
$$
