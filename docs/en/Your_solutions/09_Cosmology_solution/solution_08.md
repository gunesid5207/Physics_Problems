# 8. Cosmic Expansion

Given:

$$
d = 100\,\mathrm{million\,ly}
$$

$$
H_0 = 70\,\mathrm{(km/s)/Mpc}
$$

Convert distance to megaparsecs:

$$
1\,\mathrm{Mpc} \approx 3.26\,\mathrm{million\,ly}
$$

$$
d =
\frac{100}{3.26}
$$

$$
d \approx 30.7\,\mathrm{Mpc}
$$

Using Hubble's law:

$$
v = H_0 d
$$

$$
v = 70(30.7)
$$

$$
v \approx 2147\,\mathrm{km/s}
$$

The time is:

$$
t = 1\,\mathrm{billion\,years}
$$

Convert time to seconds:

$$
t =
10^9(365.25)(24)(3600)
$$

$$
t \approx 3.156 \times 10^{16}\,\mathrm{s}
$$

Distance increase:

$$
\Delta d = vt
$$

$$
\Delta d =
(2147)(3.156 \times 10^{16})
$$

$$
\Delta d \approx 6.78 \times 10^{19}\,\mathrm{km}
$$

Convert to light-years:

$$
1\,\mathrm{ly} \approx 9.46 \times 10^{12}\,\mathrm{km}
$$

$$
\Delta d =
\frac{6.78 \times 10^{19}}{9.46 \times 10^{12}}
$$

$$
\Delta d \approx 7.16 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{ly}
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{
\Delta d \approx 7.16 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{ly}
}
$$

So the distance increases by about:

$$
\boxed{
7.16\,\mathrm{million\,light-years}
}
$$

This is about:

$$
\boxed{
7.16\%
}
$$

of the original distance.
