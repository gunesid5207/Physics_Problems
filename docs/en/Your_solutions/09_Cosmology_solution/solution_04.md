# 4. Hubble's Law and Galactic Year

## A. Hubble's Law

Given:

$$
v = H_0 d
$$

$$
H_0 = 70\,\mathrm{(km/s)/Mpc}
$$

The galaxy is receding at the speed of light:

$$
v = c = 3.00 \times 10^5\,\mathrm{km/s}
$$

Solve for distance:

$$
d = \frac{v}{H_0}
$$

Substitute:

$$
d =
\frac{3.00 \times 10^5}{70}
$$

$$
d \approx 4286\,\mathrm{Mpc}
$$

Since:

$$
1\,\mathrm{Mpc} \approx 3.26 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{ly}
$$

Convert to light-years:

$$
d \approx 4286(3.26 \times 10^6)
$$

$$
d \approx 1.40 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{ly}
$$

---

## B. Galactic Year

Given:

$$
r = 27000\,\mathrm{ly}
$$

$$
v = 220\,\mathrm{km/s}
$$

Convert radius to kilometers:

$$
1\,\mathrm{ly} \approx 9.46 \times 10^{12}\,\mathrm{km}
$$

$$
r =
27000(9.46 \times 10^{12})
$$

$$
r \approx 2.55 \times 10^{17}\,\mathrm{km}
$$

The orbit circumference is:

$$
C = 2\pi r
$$

$$
C \approx 2\pi(2.55 \times 10^{17})
$$

$$
C \approx 1.60 \times 10^{18}\,\mathrm{km}
$$

The time is:

$$
t = \frac{C}{v}
$$

$$
t =
\frac{1.60 \times 10^{18}}{220}
$$

$$
t \approx 7.29 \times 10^{15}\,\mathrm{s}
$$

Convert to years:

$$
t =
\frac{7.29 \times 10^{15}}{3.156 \times 10^7}
$$

$$
t \approx 2.31 \times 10^8\,\mathrm{years}
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{
d \approx 4.29 \times 10^3\,\mathrm{Mpc}
\approx 1.40 \times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{ly}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{Galactic year} \approx 2.3 \times 10^8\,\mathrm{years}
}
$$
