# 2. Cosmic Temperatures

We order the objects and eras from hottest to coldest.

Approximate temperatures:

$$
T_{\mathrm{nucleosynthesis}} \sim 10^9\,\mathrm{K}
$$

$$
T_{\mathrm{Sun\,core}} \sim 1.5 \times 10^7\,\mathrm{K}
$$

$$
T_{\mathrm{hydrogen\,ionization}} \sim 1.6 \times 10^5\,\mathrm{K}
$$

$$
T_{\mathrm{Sun\,surface}} \sim 5800\,\mathrm{K}
$$

$$
T_{\mathrm{recombination}} \sim 3000\,\mathrm{K}
$$

$$
T_{\mathrm{human}} \sim 310\,\mathrm{K}
$$

$$
T_{\mathrm{CMB}} \sim 2.7\,\mathrm{K}
$$

Interstellar space is often only a few kelvin, and a 10 solar mass black hole has an extremely tiny Hawking temperature.

---

## Final Answer

From hottest to coldest:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Universe at nucleosynthesis era} \\
&\rightarrow \text{Core of the Sun} \\
&\rightarrow \text{Ionization temperature of hydrogen} \\
&\rightarrow \text{Surface of the Sun} \\
&\rightarrow \text{Universe at recombination era} \\
&\rightarrow \text{Human body temperature} \\
&\rightarrow \text{Present CMB radiation} \\
&\rightarrow \text{Interstellar space} \\
&\rightarrow \text{10 solar mass black hole}
\end{aligned}
}
$$
