# Problem 3: Conservation of Energy

A pendulum of length

$$
L = 1.0\ \text{m}
$$

is released from an initial angle

$$
\theta = 15^\circ
$$

We want to find the speed of the pendulum bob at the bottom of its swing.

---

## 1. Use conservation of mechanical energy

At the initial position, the pendulum has gravitational potential energy and zero kinetic energy.

At the bottom, the potential energy is minimum and all of that lost potential energy becomes kinetic energy.

So,

$$
mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2
$$

The mass cancels out:

$$
gh = \frac{1}{2}v^2
$$

Thus,

$$
v = \sqrt{2gh}
$$

---

## 2. Find the vertical height difference

The bob starts at angle \(\theta\), so the height above the lowest point is

$$
h = L(1-\cos\theta)
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
h = 1.0(1-\cos 15^\circ)
$$

Using

$$
\cos 15^\circ \approx 0.9659
$$

we get

$$
h = 1 - 0.9659 = 0.0341\ \text{m}
$$

---

## 3. Calculate the speed

Now use

$$
v = \sqrt{2gh}
$$

Substitute \(g=9.81\ \text{m/s}^2\) and \(h=0.0341\ \text{m}\):

$$
v = \sqrt{2(9.81)(0.0341)}
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{0.669} \approx 0.818\ \text{m/s}
$$

So,

$$
\boxed{v \approx 0.82\ \text{m/s}}
$$

---

## Final answer

The speed of the pendulum bob at the bottom of its swing is

$$
\boxed{0.82\ \text{m/s}}
$$
