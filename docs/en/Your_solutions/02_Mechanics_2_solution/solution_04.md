# Problem 4: Energy & Momentum

A block of mass

$$
m_1 = 0.5\ \text{kg}
$$

slides down a frictionless track from a height

$$
h = 3.0\ \text{m}
$$

At the bottom, it collides and sticks to another block of mass

$$
m_2 = 1.5\ \text{kg}
$$

which is initially at rest.

We want to find the speed of the combined mass just after the collision.

---

## 1. Speed of the first block before collision

Since the track is frictionless, mechanical energy is conserved during the slide.

Initial potential energy becomes kinetic energy at the bottom:

$$
m_1gh = \frac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2
$$

The mass \(m_1\) cancels out:

$$
gh = \frac{1}{2}v_1^2
$$

So,

$$
v_1 = \sqrt{2gh}
$$

Substitute the values \(g=9.81\ \text{m/s}^2\) and \(h=3.0\ \text{m}\):

$$
v_1 = \sqrt{2(9.81)(3.0)}
$$

$$
v_1 = \sqrt{58.86} \approx 7.67\ \text{m/s}
$$

---

## 2. Use conservation of momentum for the collision

The blocks stick together, so this is a perfectly inelastic collision.

Momentum is conserved:

$$
m_1v_1 + m_2v_2 = (m_1+m_2)v_f
$$

Since the second block is initially at rest, \(v_2=0\), so:

$$
m_1v_1 = (m_1+m_2)v_f
$$

Solve for \(v_f\):

$$
v_f = \frac{m_1v_1}{m_1+m_2}
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
v_f = \frac{0.5 \times 7.67}{0.5+1.5}
$$

$$
v_f = \frac{3.835}{2.0} \approx 1.92\ \text{m/s}
$$

---

## Final answer

The speed of the combined mass just after the collision is

$$
\boxed{v_f \approx 1.92\ \text{m/s}}
$$
