# Problem 7: Dynamics with Friction

A 5 kg block is placed on a 10 kg block. A horizontal force of 45 N is applied to the 10 kg block, and the 5 kg block is tied to the wall. The coefficient of kinetic friction between all moving surfaces is 0.2.

We want to find the acceleration of the 10 kg block.

---

## 1. Forces acting on the 10 kg block

Let:

$$
m_1 = 5\ \text{kg}
$$

$$
m_2 = 10\ \text{kg}
$$

$$
F = 45\ \text{N}
$$

$$
\mu_k = 0.2
$$

$$
g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

The 10 kg block experiences:

1. The applied force \(F=45\ \text{N}\) to the right.
2. Friction from the upper 5 kg block to the left.
3. Friction from the ground to the left.

---

## 2. Friction between the 5 kg block and the 10 kg block

Since the upper block is tied to the wall, the 10 kg block slides under it.

So kinetic friction acts between them:

$$
f_1 = \mu_k N_1
$$

The normal force from the upper block is

$$
N_1 = m_1 g = 5 \times 9.81 = 49.05\ \text{N}
$$

Thus,

$$
f_1 = 0.2 \times 49.05 = 9.81\ \text{N}
$$

This friction acts to the left on the 10 kg block.

---

## 3. Friction between the 10 kg block and the ground

The ground supports both blocks, so the normal force is

$$
N_2 = (m_1 + m_2)g = (5+10)\times 9.81 = 147.15\ \text{N}
$$

Thus the friction with the ground is

$$
f_2 = \mu_k N_2
$$

$$
f_2 = 0.2 \times 147.15 = 29.43\ \text{N}
$$

This also acts to the left.

---

## 4. Net force on the 10 kg block

The total opposing friction force is

$$
f_{\text{total}} = f_1 + f_2 = 9.81 + 29.43 = 39.24\ \text{N}
$$

So the net force is

$$
F_{\text{net}} = 45 - 39.24 = 5.76\ \text{N}
$$

---

## 5. Acceleration of the 10 kg block

Using Newton's second law:

$$
F_{\text{net}} = m_2 a
$$

$$
5.76 = 10a
$$

Thus,

$$
a = \frac{5.76}{10} = 0.576\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

---

## Final answer

The acceleration of the 10 kg block is

$$
\boxed{0.576\ \text{m/s}^2}
$$

approximately

$$
\boxed{0.58\ \text{m/s}^2}
$$
