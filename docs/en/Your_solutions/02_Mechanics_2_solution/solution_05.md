# Problem 5: Inelastic Collision

A runner of mass

$$
m_1 = 70\ \text{kg}
$$

moves with speed

$$
v_1 = 3\ \text{m/s}
$$

and jumps onto a stationary cart of mass

$$
m_2 = 140\ \text{kg}
$$

We want to find the final speed of the cart with the runner and determine whether kinetic energy is conserved.

---

## 1. Use conservation of momentum

This is an inelastic collision because the runner and cart move together after the jump.

Momentum is conserved:

$$
m_1v_1 + m_2v_2 = (m_1+m_2)v_f
$$

Since the cart is initially at rest,

$$
v_2 = 0
$$

So,

$$
m_1v_1 = (m_1+m_2)v_f
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
70 \cdot 3 = (70+140)v_f
$$

$$
210 = 210v_f
$$

Thus,

$$
v_f = 1\ \text{m/s}
$$

---

## 2. Is kinetic energy conserved?

### Initial kinetic energy

Only the runner is moving initially:

$$
K_i = \frac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2
$$

$$
K_i = \frac{1}{2}(70)(3^2)
$$

$$
K_i = 35 \cdot 9 = 315\ \text{J}
$$

### Final kinetic energy

After the collision, the runner and cart move together:

$$
K_f = \frac{1}{2}(m_1+m_2)v_f^2
$$

$$
K_f = \frac{1}{2}(210)(1^2)
$$

$$
K_f = 105\ \text{J}
$$

Since

$$
K_f \ne K_i
$$

kinetic energy is not conserved.

---

## 3. Explanation

Momentum is conserved because there is no external horizontal force.

Kinetic energy is not conserved because this is an inelastic collision. Some of the mechanical energy is transformed into heat, sound, and deformation.

---

## Final answers

The final speed of the cart with the runner is

$$
\boxed{1.0\ \text{m/s}}
$$

Kinetic energy is **not conserved** in this collision.
