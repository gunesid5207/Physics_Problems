# Problem 6: Energy Dissipation

A tennis ball is dropped from a height

$$
h_0 = 2.0\ \text{m}
$$

After each bounce, it loses 30% of its mechanical energy.

We want to find the height reached after the second bounce.

---

## 1. Energy after each bounce

If the ball loses 30% of its energy after each bounce, then it keeps

$$
70\% = 0.7
$$

of its energy.

Since gravitational potential energy is proportional to height,

$$
E = mgh
$$

the height after each bounce is also multiplied by \(0.7\).

---

## 2. Height after the first bounce

$$
h_1 = 0.7h_0
$$

Substitute \(h_0 = 2.0\ \text{m}\):

$$
h_1 = 0.7 \times 2.0 = 1.4\ \text{m}
$$

---

## 3. Height after the second bounce

Again multiply by \(0.7\):

$$
h_2 = 0.7h_1
$$

$$
h_2 = 0.7 \times 1.4 = 0.98\ \text{m}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
h_2 = 2.0(0.7)^2 = 2.0(0.49) = 0.98\ \text{m}
$$

---

## Final answer

The ball rises to a height of

$$
\boxed{0.98\ \text{m}}
$$

after the second bounce.
