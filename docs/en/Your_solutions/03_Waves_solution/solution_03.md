# Problem 3: Superposition Principle

Two waves are given:

$$
y_1(x,t) = A \sin(kx - \omega t)
$$

$$
y_2(x,t) = A \sin(kx + \omega t)
$$

## Resulting wave

Using the identity:

$$
\sin a + \sin b = 2 \sin\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{a-b}{2}\right)
$$

we obtain:

$$
y(x,t) = y_1 + y_2 = 2A \sin(kx)\cos(\omega t)
$$

This is a standing wave.

## Nodes

Nodes occur where the displacement is always zero:

$$
\sin(kx) = 0
$$

So,

$$
kx = n\pi \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = \frac{n\pi}{k}
$$

Using $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$:

$$
x = n \frac{\lambda}{2}
$$

## Final Answer

Standing wave:

$$
\boxed{y(x,t) = 2A \sin(kx)\cos(\omega t)}
$$

Nodes:

$$
\boxed{x = n\frac{\lambda}{2}}, \quad n = 0,1,2,\dots
$$
