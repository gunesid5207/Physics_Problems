# 6. EM Wave Analysis

Given:

$$
E_y(x,t) = 100\sin(10^7x-\omega t)\,\mathrm{V/m}
$$

This has the standard wave form:

$$
E_y(x,t) = E_0\sin(kx-\omega t)
$$

So:

$$
k = 10^7\,\mathrm{rad/m}
$$

Because the phase is $kx-\omega t$, the wave propagates in the positive $x$ direction.

## Direction of Propagation

$$
\boxed{+x\text{ direction}}
$$

## Wavelength

Use:

$$
k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}
$$

$$
\lambda = \frac{2\pi}{k}
$$

$$
\lambda = \frac{2\pi}{10^7}
$$

$$
\lambda = 6.28\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}
$$

$$
\boxed{\lambda = 6.28\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}}
$$

## Angular Frequency

For an electromagnetic wave in vacuum:

$$
\omega = ck
$$

$$
\omega = (3.00\times10^8)(10^7)
$$

$$
\omega = 3.00\times10^{15}\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$

$$
\boxed{\omega = 3.00\times10^{15}\,\mathrm{rad/s}}
$$

## Magnetic Field Component

For an electromagnetic wave:

$$
B_0 = \frac{E_0}{c}
$$

$$
B_0 = \frac{100}{3.00\times10^8}
$$

$$
B_0 = 3.33\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T}
$$

The electric field is in the $y$ direction and the wave propagates in the $+x$ direction.

Since:

$$
\vec{E}\times\vec{B}
$$

must point in the $+x$ direction, the magnetic field must be in the $-z$ direction.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{B_z(x,t) = -3.33\times10^{-7}\sin(10^7x-3.00\times10^{15}t)\,\mathrm{T}}
$$
